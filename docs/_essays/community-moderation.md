---
title: Moderation Is Care, Not Authority
topic: DevRel
updated: 2026-04-29
description: "How I moderate developer communities: give far more than you ask, let hard threads run, and start with the smallest action that works."
---

Trust is what you're protecting.

A moderator's job is to keep a community somewhere people still want to talk to each other. Enforcement is the small, visible part of that. I've done this work in Auth0's developer community and in OpenFGA's.

Most of the work looks nothing like moderation. It's answering questions, unblocking people, and pointing at good work by other members. A useful ratio is nine helpful interactions for every one where you ask the community for something. When someone does something generous, thank them where everyone can see it. Trust builds slowly this way, and it's what you spend when you finally have to act.

When a thread gets tense, wait. Some friction is how a community works things out, and stepping in early teaches people to stop saying what they think. Dogpiling is the signal to act. By then the original disagreement has dropped away and people are only piling on. Close the thread, and say where follow-up should go.

When you do act, start with the smallest thing that works. Explain what you did and which rule it falls under, because people accept consequences they understand and resent ones that feel arbitrary. Mute before you block. A mute calms things down without handing an upset member a grievance. Take anything personal to a DM or email, since settling someone's situation in public costs trust you spent months earning.

Harassment is the exception to all of that patience. Act on it fast. Protecting the people in the room is why the role exists.

Most of this is invisible when it's going well. People relax and take part, and nobody thinks about the moderator.
